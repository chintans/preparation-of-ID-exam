## Lesson 1: Tenure, reappointment, cooling-off, rotation (A4)

### The rules

| Point | Provision | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| First term | Sec 149(10) | Up to **5 consecutive years**. |
| Second term | Sec 149(10) | Reappointment needs a **special resolution**, and the board's report must disclose it. |
| Cap | Sec 149(11) | No more than **2 consecutive terms**. |
| Cooling-off | Sec 149(11) | Reappointment is possible only after **3 years** out. During those 3 years the person must not be appointed in, or associated with, the company **in any other capacity, directly or indirectly**. |
| Counting | Explanation to Sec 149(10)/(11) | Only tenure from **1 April 2014** onwards counts as a "term". |
| Rotation | Sec 149(13) | Retirement by rotation under Sec 152(6) and (7) does **not** apply to IDs. IDs are also left out of the "total number of directors" used for the rotation calculation (Explanation to Sec 152(6)). |
| First appointment | Sec 150(2), Sec 152(5) | Approved by shareholders at a general meeting. The explanatory statement must say why the board thinks the person meets the independence criteria. Under the Act, an ordinary resolution is enough. |
| Removal of a 2nd-term ID | Sec 169(1) proviso | Needs a **special resolution**, and the ID must get a reasonable opportunity to be heard. |
| Listed companies | LODR Reg 25(2A) | **Appointment, reappointment and removal** of an ID all need a **special resolution**. |
| Alternate route (listed) | Reg 25(2A) proviso (Nov 2022 amendment) | If a special resolution to **appoint** an ID fails, the ID is still **deemed appointed** when (a) votes for exceed votes against **and** (b) public shareholders' votes for exceed their votes against. An ID appointed this way can be removed only by the same two-part test. |
| Max ID seats (listed) | LODR Reg 25(1) | At most **7 listed entities** as ID. If you are a **whole-time director or MD of any listed entity**, at most **3** listed entities as ID. |
| ID turning executive | LODR Reg 25(11) | An ID who resigns cannot become a whole-time director of the same listed entity, its holding, subsidiary or associate, or a promoter-group company, until **1 year** after the resignation. |
| Vacancy | Rule 4(2) AQD Rules; LODR Reg 25(6) | The board must fill it at the earliest, and **no later than the next board meeting or 3 months from the vacancy, whichever is later**. For a listed company, no replacement is needed if the board still meets the ID requirement without one. |

### Worked examples

**Case 1: the cooling-off clock.** Ms Rao was an ID of Alpha Ltd from 1 Apr 2016 to 31 Mar 2021 and was reappointed by special resolution until 31 Mar 2026. She has now served 2 consecutive terms. The earliest she can return as an ID is **1 Apr 2029**. Between Apr 2026 and Mar 2029 she cannot be Alpha's consultant, adviser, non-independent director or employee. Any of these would mean being "associated in any other capacity" and would break the cooling-off.

**Case 2: deemed approval.** Beta Ltd, a listed company, proposes Mr Khan as an ID. Overall, 70% of votes are in favour and 30% against, so the special resolution fails because it needed 75%. Among public shareholders, 6 crore votes are in favour and 4 crore against. Both proviso tests are met (70 > 30 overall, and 6 > 4 among the public), so Mr Khan is **deemed appointed**.

**Case 3: rotation.** Gamma Ltd has 9 directors, 3 of whom are IDs. The rotation base is the **6** non-IDs. At least 2/3 of them, i.e. **4**, must be rotational directors, and 1/3 of those (1.33, so 1) retire at each AGM. The 3 IDs are outside this calculation.

### Common exam traps
- The Act lets you appoint an ID for a first term by **ordinary** resolution, but LODR needs a **special** resolution for a listed company. Read the question carefully: is it about the Act or about a listed company?
- Cooling-off is **3 years with no association at all**, not just "not an ID".
- Tenure **before 1 April 2014** does not count as a term.
- The Reg 25(1) cap is **7**. It drops to **3** only for someone who is an executive (WTD/MD) **of a listed entity**.
- For vacancies, the deadline is the **later** of the next board meeting and 3 months, not the earlier.

## Lesson 2: Declarations and the Code for IDs, Schedule IV (A5)

### Declarations
- **Sec 149(7):** every ID gives a declaration that they meet the Sec 149(6) criteria. It is due **(i)** at the first board meeting they attend as a director, **(ii)** at the first board meeting of **every financial year**, and **(iii)** whenever circumstances change in a way that may affect their independence.
- **Rule 6(3), AQD Rules:** alongside the Sec 149(7) declaration, the ID also declares that they comply with the **databank registration** rules (Rule 6(1) and (2)).
- **LODR Reg 25(8):** a listed company's ID must also confirm they know of no circumstance, existing or reasonably foreseeable, that could impair their ability to act objectively. **Reg 25(9):** the board takes the declaration on record **after assessing its veracity**.

### Schedule IV, the Code for Independent Directors (Sec 149(8))
The company and its IDs must both follow it. It has eight parts:

| Part | What it covers (key points) |
|---|---|
| I Professional conduct | Uphold ethical standards, act objectively, devote enough time, avoid conflicts, don't abuse the position for personal gain. |
| II Role & functions | Bring independent judgement on strategy, performance, risk and key appointments; balance the interests of all stakeholders, including minority shareholders. |
| III Duties | Attend board and committee meetings and general meetings; take training; raise concerns about unethical behaviour or fraud; make sure the vigil mechanism works; don't disclose confidential information; make sure **related party transactions** get proper deliberation. |
| IV Manner of appointment | The selection process is independent of management. A **formal letter of appointment** sets out the terms, and those terms are posted on the company's website. |
| V Reappointment | Based on the **performance evaluation report**. |
| VI Resignation / removal | As per the Act. |
| VII Separate meetings | IDs meet **at least once a financial year** with **no non-IDs and no management present** (also LODR Reg 25(3)). They review the non-IDs and the board as a whole, review the **chairperson** (taking the views of executive and non-executive directors into account), and assess the **quality, quantity and timeliness of information** reaching the board. |
| VIII Evaluation | The **entire board, excluding the director being evaluated**, evaluates each ID. The result decides whether to extend or continue the term. |

Other LODR items worth knowing: **Reg 25(7)** requires familiarisation programmes for IDs, with details on the company's website. **Reg 25(10)** requires D&O insurance for IDs in the **top 1,000 listed entities by market capitalisation**.

### Worked example
Delta Ltd's IDs hold their separate meeting with the CFO present "to answer questions". **That is not valid.** Management may not attend. The IDs should hold the meeting on their own and, if they need information, ask for it before or after.

At that meeting the IDs want to approve a large related party transaction. **They can't do it there.** RPT approval belongs to the audit committee (Sec 177, LODR Reg 23). The separate meeting only reviews and assesses.

### Common exam traps
- Declarations are due **annually**, at the first board meeting of each financial year, not at every meeting.
- The separate meeting has **no decision-making role**. It reviews performance and information flow.
- In the evaluation, the ID being evaluated **sits out**. The rest of the board, not only the NRC, does the evaluation.
