## Lesson 1 – The exam: who takes it, format, exemptions

### Concept
Every independent director (ID) must be registered in the **ID Databank** maintained by IICA (Sec 150(1) read with Rule 6 of the Companies (Appointment and Qualification of Directors) Rules, 2014, the "AQD Rules"). After registering, an ID must **pass an online proficiency self-assessment test** unless an exemption applies.

### The provisions
- **Rule 6(1):** An existing ID must apply to IICA for databank inclusion. A person who **intends** to be appointed as an ID must apply **before** appointment. Registration is for 1 year, 5 years or lifetime, and can be renewed. (Fees are charged per registration plan; check the current amounts on the portal.)
- **Rule 6(3):** Every ID must file a declaration of compliance with Rule 6(1) and (2) with the Board each time they submit the Sec 149(7) declaration of independence.
- **Rule 6(4):** The ID must pass the test **within 2 years** of their name being included in the databank. If they don't, the name is **removed** from the databank. The pass mark is **50% in aggregate**.
  - Both figures come from the 18 Dec 2020 amendment. Before that, the window was 1 year and the pass mark was 60%. Exam questions sometimes use the old figures as distractors.
- **Format:** 50 MCQs, 100 marks (2 each), 75 minutes, **no negative marking**. The questions are knowledge-based plus scenario-based. You get **unlimited attempts**, with a 1-day gap between slot bookings. There are two slots a day (2–3 PM and 8–9 PM).
- **Syllabus:** Companies law, securities law, basic accountancy and corporate governance.

### Exemptions (proviso to Rule 6(4))
You don't need to pass the test if, **on the date of inclusion in the databank**, any of these applies:

1. You have served **at least 3 years in total** as a **director or KMP** in any of the following:
   - a **listed** public company, whatever its capital;
   - an **unlisted public company with paid-up share capital ≥ ₹10 crore**;
   - a body corporate listed on a recognised stock exchange in an FATF member country whose securities regulator is an IOSCO member;
   - a foreign body corporate with paid-up capital **≥ US$ 2 million**;
   - a statutory corporation set up under a Central or State Act that carries on commercial activities.
   Periods served **at the same time** in two or more entities count **only once**.
2. You have served in Central or State Government at **Director level or above**, with experience in commerce, corporate affairs, finance, industry or public enterprises.
3. You have served in **SEBI, RBI, IRDAI or PFRDA** at **Chief General Manager level or above**, with experience in corporate, securities or economic laws.
4. You have practised for **10+ years** as an **advocate, CA, CMA (cost accountant) or Company Secretary**.

### Worked examples
- **Ms Rao** was a director of a *private* company with ₹50 crore paid-up capital for 6 years. **Not exempt.** A private company isn't in the list, however large it is.
- **Mr Iyer** was CFO (a KMP) of a listed company from Apr 2023 to Mar 2025, and at the same time director of an unlisted public company (₹15 crore paid-up) from Apr 2024 to Mar 2025. Total service = **2 years**, because the overlapping year counts once. **Not exempt.**
- **Mr Shah** has been a practising CA for 12 years. **Exempt** (10+ years of practice).

### Common exam traps
- **3 years**, not 10, for the director/KMP exemption. 10 years applies **only** to the professional-practice route.
- The ₹10 crore paid-up test applies **only to unlisted** public companies.
- Exemptions are tested **on the date of inclusion in the databank**, not on the date of appointment.
- Missing the 2-year deadline leads to **removal from the databank**. It is not a fine.

## Lesson 2 – Which companies must appoint independent directors?

### The provisions
- **Sec 149(4):** Every **listed public company** must have **at least one-third** of its total directors as IDs. Any fraction is rounded **up** to one (Explanation to 149(4)).
- **Rule 4(1), AQD Rules:** These **public companies** must have **at least 2 IDs**:
  1. paid-up share capital **≥ ₹10 crore**; or
  2. turnover **≥ ₹100 crore**; or
  3. aggregate outstanding **loans, debentures and deposits > ₹50 crore**.
  Each test applies to **any one** of these, measured on the **latest audited financial statements**.
- **Exempt from Rule 4** (these must be *unlisted* public companies): **joint ventures, wholly owned subsidiaries, and dormant companies** (Sec 455).
- **Rule 4(2):** If a company stops meeting all the thresholds for **3 consecutive years**, it doesn't need to comply until it meets any of them again.
- **Private companies** never need IDs under the Act.
- **Listed entities, additional SEBI rule: LODR Reg 17(1)(b):**
  - If the chairperson is a **non-executive** director, at least **one-third** of the board must be IDs.
  - If there is **no regular non-executive chairperson**, or the non-executive chairperson is a **promoter or related to a promoter** (or to anyone at board level or one level below), at least **half** of the board must be IDs.
  - **Reg 17(1)(a):** At least one **woman ID** on the board of the **top 1,000** listed entities by market capitalisation. The board of the **top 2,000** must have **at least 6 directors** (Reg 17(1)(c)).
- When the Act and LODR both apply, the company must meet the **stricter** of the two.

### Worked examples
1. **Company X** is an unlisted public company with paid-up capital of **₹12 crore**, turnover of ₹40 crore and no borrowings. **Yes, it needs 2 IDs**, because paid-up capital is ≥ ₹10 crore. If X were a **wholly owned subsidiary** of another company, it would be **exempt**.
2. **Company Y** is an unlisted public company with paid-up capital of ₹4 crore and turnover of ₹80 crore. It has term loans of ₹30 crore and debentures of ₹25 crore, so outstanding loans plus debentures = **₹55 crore > ₹50 crore**. **Yes, it needs 2 IDs.**
3. **Company Z Pvt Ltd** has a turnover of ₹900 crore. **It needs no IDs**, because it is a private company.
4. **Listed company L** has 8 directors, and its chairperson is a **non-executive promoter**.
   - Under the Companies Act: 1/3 × 8 = 2.67, which rounds up to **3**.
   - Under LODR: the promoter chair triggers the half rule, so ½ × 8 = **4**.
   - The stricter rule applies, so L needs **4 IDs**.

### Common exam traps
- Paid-up capital and turnover use "**≥**". Borrowings use "**exceeding**" (> ₹50 crore).
- The JV / WOS / dormant exemption is in **Rule 4** and covers **unlisted public** companies only.
- With 1/3 of 10 directors, the answer is **4**, not 3, because fractions round up.
- The minimum under Rule 4 is "**at least two**" IDs, not one-third.

## Lesson 3 – Who is "independent"? Sec 149(6)

### The provision
An ID is a director **other than** a managing director, whole-time director or nominee director, who meets **all** of these conditions:

| Clause | Test |
|---|---|
| (a) | In the Board's opinion, is a person of **integrity** with relevant expertise and experience |
| (b) | (i) Is **not a promoter**, and has never been one, of the company or its holding, subsidiary or associate company. (ii) Is **not related** to the promoters or directors of any of these. |
| (c) | Has (or had) **no pecuniary relationship** with the company, its holding/subsidiary/associate company, or their promoters or directors, beyond remuneration as a director or transactions up to **10% of the person's total income**. This covers the **2 immediately preceding FYs or the current FY**. |
| (d) | **Relatives:** No relative holds securities above a set limit (face value of ₹50 lakh or 2% of paid-up capital), is indebted to the company or has given a guarantee/security above **₹50 lakh** (Rule 5), or has any other pecuniary transaction amounting to **2% or more** of the company's gross turnover or total income. The period is the 2 preceding FYs or the current FY. |
| (e) | **Neither the person nor any relative:** (i) has been **KMP or an employee** of the company or its group in any of the **3 preceding FYs**. Proviso: for a relative who is an **employee**, this restriction doesn't apply. (ii) has been an employee, proprietor or partner in the **statutory audit firm, secretarial audit (practising CS) firm or cost audit firm** of the company or its group, **or** in a legal or consulting firm whose dealings with the company/group are **≥ 10% of that firm's gross turnover**, in any of the 3 preceding FYs. (iii) holds, together with relatives, **2% or more of the total voting power**. (iv) is the CEO or a director of an **NPO** that gets **≥ 25% of its receipts** from the company, its promoters, directors or group, or that holds **≥ 2% of voting power**. |
| (f) | Has any other qualification prescribed in **Rule 5** (expertise in areas such as law, finance, management, sales, marketing, administration, research, technical operations, etc.) |

"Relative" has the meaning in **Sec 2(76)**: members of a HUF, spouse, father, mother, son, son's wife, daughter, daughter's husband, brother and sister (Rule 4 of the Specification of Definitions Rules).

**Under LODR Reg 16(1)(b),** a listed entity's ID must also:
- be **at least 21 years old**;
- not be a **material supplier, service provider or customer**, or a lessor/lessee, of the company;
- not be part of a **board interlock**: they must not be a non-independent director of another company on whose board a non-independent director of the listed entity sits as an ID.

LODR also has its own cooling-off rule for former KMP/employees and their relatives.

### Mini case studies
1. **Ms A** is a partner in the CA firm that was **statutory auditor of the company's subsidiary** until FY 2024-25. She is proposed as ID in FY 2026-27. FY 2024-25 is within the 3 preceding FYs, so she is **not independent** (149(6)(e)(ii)).
2. **Mr B** and his wife together hold **2.4%** of the voting power. He is **not independent** (149(6)(e)(iii), which counts holdings together with relatives).
3. **Mr C** was **CFO of the holding company** until 2 years ago. He is **not independent**: he was a KMP of the holding company within the 3 preceding FYs (149(6)(e)(i)).
4. **Dr D's son** is a junior **employee** of the company. Under the proviso to 149(6)(e)(i), a relative's employment doesn't disqualify Dr D. Dr D **can be independent**, provided every other test is met.
5. **Mr E** is the Managing Director of the company. He can **never** be an ID. The definition excludes MDs, WTDs and **nominee directors**.

### Common exam traps
- Look-back periods: **2 preceding FYs + the current FY** for pecuniary relationships (c, d), and **3 preceding FYs** for employment and auditor/consultant links (e).
- **10%** of the ID's own income (c), **2%** of the company's turnover (d), **2%** of voting power (e)(iii), and **25%** of an NPO's receipts (e)(iv). Don't mix these up.
- A **nominee director** is never independent.
- It is the **Board** that forms the opinion on integrity and expertise under 149(6)(a), not the shareholders.
